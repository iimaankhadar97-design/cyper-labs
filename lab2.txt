LAB 2: Cyber Incident Impact Mapping

Scenario: Fictional University Cyber Breach

A fictional university has experienced a cyberattack in which an unauthorized person gained access to the university’s student management system.
The attacker may have accessed student personal information, academic records, and staff account details. 
The university must identify the impact, contain the incident, preserve evidence, and restore critical service.
 
IMPACT MAP

Area | Immediate Consequence | Secondary Consequence

Student Database: Unauthorized access to student records/ Privacy violations and loss of student trust
Student Accounts:  Some accounts may be compromised  / Students may be unable to access university services
Academic Records:  Grades and academic information may be exposed or changed  Incorrect academic decisions and administrative problems
Staff Accounts: Staff credentials may be compromised  / Attackers may gain access to other university systems
University Server:  System may become unavailable / Disruption of university operations
Registration System: Students may be unable to register for courses / Delays in enrollment and academic activities
Financial System:  Financial information may be exposed / Financial loss and possible fraud
University Reputation:  Students and staff may lose confidence / Damage to the university’s reputation
IT Department: IT staff must investigate and contain the breach / Increased workload and recovery costs

2. Affected Users

The following users may be affected by the incident:

1. Students – Their personal information, grades, and academic records may be exposed.
2. Teachers/Lecturers – Their accounts and academic information may be affected.
3. University Administrators – They may lose access to important management systems.
4. IT Staff – They must investigate, contain, and recover the affected systems.
5. Finance Staff – Financial information and payment systems may be affected.
6. University Management – They may face operational, financial, and reputational consequences.

3. Business Functions Affected

Business Function              Impact
Student Registration  Students may not be able to register for courses
Examination and Grading  Grades and academic records may be unavailable or incorrect
Student Records Management  Personal and academic information may be compromised
Finance and Payments  Financial records and transactions may be affected
Communication  University email and communication systems may be disrupted
IT Operations  IT staff must investigate and restore systems
Administration  Administrative activities may be delayed


4.Three Containment Actions

Action 1: Isolate the compromised systems

Immediately disconnect the affected server or system from the network to prevent the attacker from spreading to other systems.

Action 2: Disable compromised accounts

Temporarily disable affected user accounts and reset passwords to prevent unauthorized access.

Action 3: Block malicious network activity

Block suspicious IP addresses, connections, and other known malicious activity using firewalls or other security controls.

5. Student Questions

13. What information is sensitive?

Sensitive information may include:

 Student names and identification numbers
 Student contact information
 Passwords and account credentials
 Academic records and grades
 Financial and payment information
 Staff personal information
 University system credentials

Answer:
The most sensitive information includes passwords, personal information, academic records, financial information, and system credentials because unauthorized access to this information can cause privacy violations, identity theft, fraud, or further attacks.



14. What service should be restored first?

Answer:
The Student Management System and its database should be restored first because it is a critical service for accessing student records, registration, academic information, and other essential university operations.

However, the system should only be restored after confirming that it is secure and that the attacker no longer has access.



15. What evidence should be preserved?

The following evidence should be preserved:

 Server and system logs
 Firewall logs
 Authentication/login logs
 Database access logs
 Network traffic records
 Suspicious files or malware
 Compromised computers or virtual machines
 User account activity
 Timestamps of suspicious activities

Answer:
All relevant logs, suspicious files, network records, and compromised systems should be preserved so that investigators can determine how the attack happened, what information was accessed, and who or what was responsible.



6. Short Incident Summary

Incident Summary:

A fictional university experienced a cyber breach in which an unauthorized attacker gained access to the student management system. The incident potentially exposed sensitive information, including student personal details, academic records, financial information, and user credentials. The breach affected students, lecturers, administrators, finance staff, and IT personnel.

The university should first isolate the compromised systems, disable affected accounts, and block malicious network activity to contain the incident. Evidence such as server logs, firewall logs, authentication records, database logs, network traffic, and suspicious files should be preserved for investigation.

After the incident has been contained and the systems have been verified as secure, the Student Management System should be restored first because it supports critical university functions such as student registration and academic record management. The university should also improve access controls, password security, monitoring, backups, and incident response procedures to reduce the risk of future attacks.
